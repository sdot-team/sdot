API — Power Diagrams ​

The geometry under the plans: power diagrams, their cells, and what speeds them up. For seeds at the same position, see Guide: Duplicates and Close Seeds.

PowerDiagram ​

python
PowerDiagram(positions=None, weights=None, *args, accelerator=None, **kwargs)

Power diagram ( Laguerre ) -- THE VIEW, and what all the storages have in common.

The cell of seed i is where its POWER DISTANCE wins:

|x - d_i|² - w_i  <=  |x - d_j|² - w_j   for every other j

Expanded, the inequality loses its |x|² on both sides and becomes a half-space: a power diagram costs exactly what a Voronoi diagram costs, one plane per rival and the same cut. Only the DIFFERENCES of weights reach the planes: "all equal" and "no weights at all" are the same object, and the Euclidean case is called Voronoi ( see Voronoi.py ).

PowerDiagram( positions, weights, ... ) does not carry a diagram: it carries its SEEDS and the convex domain that bounds them, and rebuilds what it is asked for, cell by cell, in the scratch of a work-item ( diagram/Ops.h ). This file is the CONTRACT -- what a user reads and writes: positions, weights, measures, cells, cell( i ) -- and the common trunk: the domain, the distribution, the scratch, the three kernels. HOW the seeds are laid out is up to a specialization, chosen at construction:

  • PowerDiagram_Plain -- the seeds as they came, each cell cut by the n - 1 bisectors. The floor, and what remains when the positions are a tracer;
  • PowerDiagram_Bsp -- the seeds in the order of a BSP tree ( AaBsp ), a leaf being read in one piece, and each cell cut only by the seeds the tree could not rule out. The default as soon as the positions are concrete.

The neighborhood is ACCELERABLE, not the result: an accelerator can only silence cuts that would have removed nothing, so the cells are the SAME, up to rounding errors.

Attribute
cellsAll the cells, as one Cell batched over the seeds ( in the order of positions ) -- to draw or inspect them. With a periodicity, each cell is the whole cell of its seed, around the seed as it was given: it can lie outside the domain, and away from the others ( add_to_viz shows its copies by the group too ).
groupsThe Groups of this diagram ( groups.py ), in the user's order -- None without groups.
measuresThe measure of each cell, [ n ] -- its integral against distribution when there is one. Differentiable with respect to the positions, the weights and the parameters of the distribution.
moments( masses, first, second ): for each cell, int rho, int x rho ( [ n, d ] ) and int |x|^2 rho. Not differentiable ( see moment ).
sphere_areasWith a sink ( partial transport ): the measure of the SPHERE inside each cell, where the density is not zero -- a length in 2D, an area in 3D, [ n ]; zeros without a sink. What a surface energy counts. Differentiable like measures.

.cell_complex(contract=True) ​

The cells glued along their faces ( CellComplex ): each vertex, edge, ..., facet once, numbered per dimension, with its children ( the faces on its boundary ) and its parents ( the faces it bounds ) -- to walk the geometry of the diagram. The facets know their two seeds, or their seed and the wall of the domain ( walls, the half-spaces of domain_outline ). A host-side snapshot: a new one after moving the seeds.

With a periodicity, the complex of the QUOTIENT ( a torus, a cylinder... ): a facet across the period is there once, between a seed and an image of another -- or of itself --, and the geometry of each face is read off one of its copies, next to the given position of a seed of one of its cells ( walls: those that the group leaves, in the frame of the folded seeds ).

The cells are cut for it with CANONICAL PREDICATES ( predicates = "canonical", in doubles ), whatever the diagram's: every cell takes the same decision about the same vertex, so they agree exactly on what they share, and the gluing is pure connectivity -- no tolerance. Where the diagram is degenerate ( a grid of seeds ), the ties are broken by a symbolic perturbation, which blows a degenerate point up into vertices joined by edges of zero length. contract ( the default ): these edges are contracted, and the faces of zero measure with them -- an exact grid has the exact number of faces, its vertices shared by 2^d cells. contract = False: the complex of the perturbed diagram, in general position ( each vertex in exactly d + 1 cells, faces of zero measure included ).

Groups ​

python
Groups(parent, agg_parent, center, scale, outer, dirs=None, seed_v=None, agg_v=None, positions=None, apex_shift=None)

THE TREE OF AGGREGATES of a diagram, in the user's order. Aggregates are numbered top-down ( a parent before its children ).

Per seed: parent ( [ n ] ints, the aggregate the seed is a child of, -1 when it is alone ), dirs ( [ n, d ], the unit direction of a seed in an aggregate of exact duplicates, zero elsewhere ). Per aggregate: agg_parent ( [ m ], -1 for a root ), center ( [ m, d ], the origin of its frame -- the barycentre of its diracs, or the apex of a fan of duplicates, which follows its site: apex_shift, read from positions ), scale ( [ m ], the scale of its frame -- r for duplicates ), outer ( [ m ], what its children's weights move outside: scale, or 0 for duplicates ). Optional precise virtual weights seed_v ( [ n ] ) and agg_v ( [ m ] ), each child's v in its parent's frame ( a solver's ): without them, they are read from the weights given to the diagram ( virtual_weights ).

Host arrays: the tree is a constant of the positions it was built on ( a derivative goes through the positions of the seeds, not through the tree ). Built by find_groups( positions ) ( groups = "auto", the solver ), from nested index lists ( Groups.nested, or the lists themselves as PowerDiagram( groups = ... ) ), or one level from representatives ( Groups.from_rep ).

Attribute
apex_shiftper aggregate of duplicates: its apex minus the position of its site ( the fan follows the site ), zero for the others
depththe largest number of nested aggregates above a seed
nb_groupsthe ROOT aggregates ( the groups of seeds that share one outer cell )
repper seed: the smallest index of its root aggregate ( itself when it is alone )
siteper seed: the smallest index at its exact position ( the first seed of its aggregate of duplicates, or itself )

CellComplex ​

python
CellComplex(cells, walls=None, seeds=None, weights=None, power=True, group=None, maps=None, values=None, fold=None, contract=True)

The cells of a diagram glued along their faces: every face once, numbered per dimension, with its incidences. Built by PowerDiagram.cell_complex() -- or from any batch of cells, CellComplex( cells, walls ), the items being the seeds their cut identifiers designate.

faces( k ) the k-faces, vertices / edges / facets / cells those of dimension 0, 1, d - 1, d. walls: the half-spaces ( directions, offsets ) of the domain, which the negative sides of the facets designate ( -1 - wall ). Cells cut with canonical predicates ( Cell_C ) glue exactly; others are glued by their keys alone, and the check says whether they agreed ( is_consistent ). contract: the edges of zero length that the cells mark ( vertex_ties: a degenerate point blown up by the tie-breaking ) are contracted -- False keeps the complex of the perturbed diagram, in general position.

Attribute
cellsThe dim-faces, numbered like the seeds ( an empty cell has no face ).
diagnosticsWhat the check of the gluing found ( see _check ): one_sided_facets, open_links, kept_zero_edges ( the edges of zero length that were not contracted, an elongated sliver in conflict ), and facet_mismatch ( computed when read: the measures of the copies of every facet ).
dimThe dimension of the space ( and of the cells ).
edgesThe 1-faces ( the cells themselves in 1D ).
facetsThe ( dim - 1 )-faces: what two cells share, or a cell and a wall of the domain ( Faces.sides ).
is_consistentWhether every facet between two seeds is in both their cells and the facets close around every face of dimension d - 2 ( see diagnostics: one_sided_facets, open_links, facet_mismatch ). Always, for the cells of a diagram.
vertex_positions[ nb_vertices, d ]: the position of each vertex ( with a periodicity, of one of its copies ) -- a tensor of the framework of the positions, differentiable ( geometry ).
verticesThe 0-faces.

.locate(points) ​

( cells [ m ], moved [ m, d ] ): the cell that holds each point ( -1 outside the domain ), and the point moved by the group to the copy of that cell ( Face.points ) -- the point itself without a periodicity, a tensor of its framework. Exact: the cell of the smallest power distance, found by a nearest-neighbour search among the seeds lifted in one more dimension ( |x - p|^2 - w = |( x, 0 ) - ( p, sqrt( W - w ) )|^2 - W ), and among their images with a periodicity.

.nb_faces(k) ​

The number of k-faces.

.simplices(k=None, ids=None) ​

A decomposition of the k-faces ( the cells by default; ids: those faces only ) into k-simplices: ( points [ m, k + 1, d ], faces [ m ] ), the corners of each simplex and the face it belongs to. Each face is fanned from the mean of its vertices over its faces one dimension below, themselves fanned from theirs, down to the edges: a simplex per chain face > ... > edge. Exact: the volumes of the simplices of a face add up to its measure. Unbounded faces have none; with a periodicity, the simplices of a face are those of the copy its geometry is read off ( Face.points ). points: a tensor of the framework of the positions, differentiable.

AaBsp(positions, weights=None, max_seeds_per_leaf=10)

A tree of boxes over the seeds of a power diagram, which spares each cell the seeds that cannot cut it. The diagrams build one by themselves ( the default as soon as the positions are not traced ); AaBsp.of( power_diagram ) builds one explicitly, to give as accelerator.

.of(power_diagram, max_seeds_per_leaf=10) ​

The accelerator of the seeds of power_diagram -- its positions AND its weights.

The shortcut one almost always wants: a BSP built on other weights than those of the diagram would remain CORRECT (the majorant would only prune less well) but would have no reason to be good.
