API — Power Diagrams ​

The geometry under the plans: power diagrams, their cells, and what speeds them up. For seeds at the same position, see Guide: Duplicates and Close Seeds.

PowerDiagram ​

python
PowerDiagram(positions=None, weights=None, *args, accelerator=None, **kwargs)

Power diagram ( Laguerre ) -- THE VIEW, and what all the storages have in common.

The cell of seed i is where its POWER DISTANCE wins:

|x - d_i|² - w_i  <=  |x - d_j|² - w_j   for every other j

Expanded, the inequality loses its |x|² on both sides and becomes a half-space: a power diagram costs exactly what a Voronoi diagram costs, one plane per rival and the same cut. Only the DIFFERENCES of weights reach the planes: "all equal" and "no weights at all" are the same object, and the Euclidean case is called Voronoi ( see Voronoi.py ).

PowerDiagram( positions, weights, ... ) does not carry a diagram: it carries its SEEDS and the convex domain that bounds them, and rebuilds what it is asked for, cell by cell, in the scratch of a work-item ( diagram/Ops.h ). This file is the CONTRACT -- what a user reads and writes: positions, weights, measures, cells, cell( i ) -- and the common trunk: the domain, the distribution, the scratch, the three kernels. HOW the seeds are laid out is up to a specialization, chosen at construction:

  • PowerDiagram_Plain -- the seeds as they came, each cell cut by the n - 1 bisectors. The floor, and what remains when the positions are a tracer;
  • PowerDiagram_Bsp -- the seeds in the order of a BSP tree ( AaBsp ), a leaf being read in one piece, and each cell cut only by the seeds the tree could not rule out. The default as soon as the positions are concrete.

The neighborhood is ACCELERABLE, not the result: an accelerator can only silence cuts that would have removed nothing, so the cells are the SAME, up to rounding errors.

Attribute
cellsAll the cells, as one Cell batched over the seeds ( in the order of positions ) -- to draw or inspect them. With a periodicity, each cell is the whole cell of its seed, around the seed as it was given: it can lie outside the domain, and away from the others ( add_to_viz shows its copies by the group too ).
groupsThe Groups of this diagram ( groups.py ), in the user's order -- None without groups.
measuresThe measure of each cell, [ n ] -- its integral against distribution when there is one. Differentiable with respect to the positions, the weights and the parameters of the distribution.
moments( masses, first, second ): for each cell, int rho, int x rho ( [ n, d ] ) and int |x|^2 rho. Not differentiable ( see moment ).

.add_to_viz(viz, seeds=True, seed_color='#000000', support_only=None, threshold=0.0, nb_periodicity_rings=1, opacity_periodicity_rings=0.25, fit_periodicity_rings=False, domain=None, domain_color='#000000', opacity_beyond_balls=0.2, virtual_seeds=False, virtual_color='#888888', **kwargs) ​

Draws itself into a Visualizer: all the cells, in one call ( see cells ), and the seeds as points when seeds ( seed_color ). kwargs are those of Cell.add_to_viz ( opacity, faces, edges, ... ). The quick ways to look at it -- write_html, write_pvd, show -- are those of Displayable.

support_only: the cells only where the distribution has mass ( support_pieces, with threshold ) -- an image with zero pixels does not show its cells there. By default: yes without a periodicity, no with one ( a periodic cell is drawn whole, around its seed ).

With a periodicity, the cells are drawn again moved by every element of the group up to the ring nb_periodicity_rings ( periodicity_rings ), fainter ( opacity_periodicity_rings; 0 draws no copy ), the same seed in the same color: the copies must glue without a seam. They do not count in the scale of the scene ( the zoom fits the cells themselves ) unless fit_periodicity_rings. domain: the outline of the domain ( the support that the group repeats ), dashed, and that of its images -- by default with a periodicity only.

With a sink ( partial transport, sink_weight ), the cells are drawn whole, as they are computed, but fainter ( opacity_beyond_balls ): what is integrated -- the part of each cell inside its ball -- is drawn on top, in the same color, its arc approximated by a polygon.

With GROUPS ( groups ), the cells drawn are those of the members, which split the cell of their group; virtual_seeds adds the virtual diracs of the exact duplicates ( virtual_color ), the points their split is the power diagram of.

Groups ​

python
Groups(parent, agg_parent, center, scale, outer, dirs=None, seed_v=None, agg_v=None, positions=None, apex_shift=None)

THE TREE OF AGGREGATES of a diagram, in the user's order. Aggregates are numbered top-down ( a parent before its children ).

Per seed: parent ( [ n ] ints, the aggregate the seed is a child of, -1 when it is alone ), dirs ( [ n, d ], the unit direction of a seed in an aggregate of exact duplicates, zero elsewhere ). Per aggregate: agg_parent ( [ m ], -1 for a root ), center ( [ m, d ], the origin of its frame -- the barycentre of its diracs, or the apex of a fan of duplicates, which follows its site: apex_shift, read from positions ), scale ( [ m ], the scale of its frame -- r for duplicates ), outer ( [ m ], what its children's weights move outside: scale, or 0 for duplicates ). Optional precise virtual weights seed_v ( [ n ] ) and agg_v ( [ m ] ), each child's v in its parent's frame ( a solver's ): without them, they are read from the weights given to the diagram ( virtual_weights ).

Host arrays: the tree is a constant of the positions it was built on ( a derivative goes through the positions of the seeds, not through the tree ). Built by find_groups( positions ) ( groups = "auto", the solver ), from nested index lists ( Groups.nested, or the lists themselves as PowerDiagram( groups = ... ) ), or one level from representatives ( Groups.from_rep ).

Attribute
apex_shiftper aggregate of duplicates: its apex minus the position of its site ( the fan follows the site ), zero for the others
depththe largest number of nested aggregates above a seed
nb_groupsthe ROOT aggregates ( the groups of seeds that share one outer cell )
repper seed: the smallest index of its root aggregate ( itself when it is alone )
siteper seed: the smallest index at its exact position ( the first seed of its aggregate of duplicates, or itself )

.as_lists() ​

the tree as nested index lists ( what Groups.nested takes ): one list per root aggregate

ONE LEVEL: rep ( [ n ] ) the smallest index of the group of each seed ( itself when alone ) -- a tree of depth one, the exact duplicates of a group in an aggregate of their own

.from_sets(sets, positions, radius=None, dirs=None, apex=None) ​

THE TREE OF A LAMINAR FAMILY of index sets ( two sets nested or disjoint; the ones that cross another are left out ): each set an aggregate inside the smallest set holding it

.members() ​

{ rep: [ members ] } for the root aggregates ( rep the smallest index )

.nested(lists, positions, radius=None, dirs=None, apex=None) ​

THE TREE OF NESTED INDEX LISTS: each list an aggregate, an int a seed, a list in a list a nested aggregate -- [ [ 0, 1 ], [ 2, [ 3, 4 ] ] ] makes two roots, the second holding seed 2 and the aggregate [ 3, 4 ]. Seeds at the same exact position must be in one aggregate of their own ( a list of duplicates only ), or they are put in one ( the duplicates among an aggregate's direct seeds ). radius / dirs ( [ n, d ] ): the radius and the unit directions of the virtual diracs of duplicates, springs and VIRTUAL_RADIUS local spacings by default; apex ( [ n, d ], read at each site's first seed ): the centres of their fans, when a point inside each site's cell is known ( the barycentre of the cell, which a solver has: at the weights of a transport a seed need not lie in its own cell ).

python
Cell(nb_dims=None, *args, **kwargs)

A convex polytope, possibly unbounded, possibly batched ( PowerDiagram.cells is a batch of cells ). Start from Cell.make_hypercube( d, origin, axes ) or Cell.make_unbounded( d ), then cut it by half-spaces.

Attribute
cut_planes( directions[ nb_cuts, d ], offsets[ nb_cuts ] ), re-read off the geometry -- the UNIT outward normal of each face and its offset. A cut without a vertex has a zero direction.
edges[ nb_edges, 2 ] : the edges, as vertex indices.
facesA list of cycles of vertex indices, one face per cut that carries one ( 2D and 3D ).
is_boundedWhether the cell is bounded.
measureThe measure of the cell: length, area, volume... Infinite ( the largest float ) for an unbounded cell. Differentiable with respect to the vertices.

.add_to_viz(viz, color=None, opacity=1.0, faces=True, edges=True, points=False, owners=None, nb_colors=None, frames=True, edge_opacity=1.0) ​

Draws itself into a Visualizer ( see sdot.viz.Visualizer, and cell_viz for what an unbounded cell drops ). Each item takes its color from its RANK IN THE BATCH, on a block reserved in advance : the color of a cell of a diagram says WHICH seed. owners: the rank to take the color from, per item, on a block of nb_colors -- the pieces of one cell ( PowerDiagram.support_pieces ) have its color. frames = False: drawn without counting in the scale of the scene ( the zoom fits what does count ). edge_opacity: that of the edges ( opacity is the faces' ).

.cut(direction, offset, cut_id=-1) ​

Intersects the cell with the half-space direction . x <= offset, IN PLACE.

direction need not be normalized : offset is the dot product it is compared against as is. Since the inputs and outputs of a loom.ffi_call are disjoint, the kernel writes into a NEW cell and the in-place update is only a rebinding. The room the cut requires is BOUNDED in advance ( _cut_capacities ) : no second pass.

AaBsp ​

python
AaBsp(positions, weights=None, max_seeds_per_leaf=10)

A tree of boxes over the seeds of a power diagram, which spares each cell the seeds that cannot cut it. The diagrams build one by themselves ( the default as soon as the positions are not traced ); AaBsp.of( power_diagram ) builds one explicitly, to give as accelerator.

.of(power_diagram, max_seeds_per_leaf=10) ​

The accelerator of the seeds of power_diagram -- its positions AND its weights.

The shortcut one almost always wants: a BSP built on other weights than those of the diagram would remain CORRECT (the majorant would only prune less well) but would have no reason to be good.
