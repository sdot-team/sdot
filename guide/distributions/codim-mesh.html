Lines and Surfaces ​

A target can be carried by a mesh M of lower dimension than the space: segments in 2D or 3D, triangles in 3D. The measure is ρ times the length ( or the area ) on M, with ρ piecewise linear on its nodes. Mesh gives one as soon as its simplices have fewer than d+1 nodes:

python
target = sdot.Mesh( nodes, triangles )                  # nodes [ n, 3 ], triangles [ m, 3 ]: a surface in 3D
target = sdot.Mesh( nodes, segments, values = rho )     # segments [ m, 2 ]: lines in 2D or 3D, rho at the nodes

M is any simplicial complex: open or closed, with boundaries, junctions, and even self-intersections. Nothing is assumed about its topology or its orientation.

What is computed ​

The cells of the transport are integrated exactly on M: the mass of a cell Li is ∫Li∩Mρ, and its barycenter, its derivatives and its Hessian are exact too.

A measure on M is hard to start from, though. A cell that does not meet M has no mass, and its weight gets no gradient, so Newton cannot move it. The solver goes through smoothed densities first,

ρ(p(x))e−d(x)2/s2,

with d the distance to M and p(x) the closest point of M. It starts from a width s of the size of the domain, then narrows it stage by stage down to width, and ends with the measure on M itself. Each stage starts from the weights of the previous one.

These densities are integrated in closed form, on the envelope of M: the generalized Voronoi diagram of the elements of M ( its simplices, their edges, their nodes ). It is cut into simplices on which d and ρ are affine. Around the edges and the nodes, where d is not affine, the envelope uses fans of sectors at most alpha wide, and over-estimates d by at most 1/cos⁡(α/2). Along M itself, d is exact, so the smoothed densities do tend to the measure on M.

When the starting point already gives a mass to every cell ( diracs on M ), the solver skips the smoothed stages.

Parameters ​

python
sdot.Mesh( nodes, simplices, values = None, width = None, alpha = 15, margin = 0.25, smoothed = False )
  • values: ρ at the nodes, ones by default;
  • width: the narrowest smoothed density, before the measure itself. By default, 10−5 times the diagonal of M. Wider, some cells of the last smoothed stage stay in layers along M without crossing it, and they would be empty for the measure;
  • alpha: the widest angle of the fans of the envelope, in degrees;
  • margin: the domain of the smoothed densities is the box of M, widened by margin times its diagonal;
  • smoothed = True: the target is the smoothed density at width rather than the measure on M.

The envelope is built once, with the target. In 3D it is the largest cost: on a laptop, 3 s for a torus of 1000 triangles, 25 s for 4000, and 45 s for the 2880 triangles of the bottle below, whose sheets come close to each other.

Quantization of a Klein bottle ​

sdot.quantization works on these targets as on any other one ( see Lloyd quantization ). Given a number of diracs, it draws them on M, by mass: the first transport then goes directly to the measure on M.

The surface is the bottle immersion of the Klein bottle in R3, with a neck a little wider than the classical one. It crosses itself where the neck enters the body, and it has no inside nor outside. 6000 diracs:

python
nodes, triangles = klein_bottle()                       # 2880 triangles, see the script below
target = sdot.Mesh( nodes, triangles )

x = sdot.quantization( target, 6000, seed = 0 )

The diracs end at the barycenters of their cells: slightly inside the curved parts of the surface, by the sagitta of a cell.

A density on the surface ​

The same bottle, with ρ growing from 0.1 at the bottom to 1 at the top of the neck: the diracs follow the density.

python
h = ( nodes[ :, 1 ] - nodes[ :, 1 ].min() ) / np.ptp( nodes[ :, 1 ] )     # y is the height
target = sdot.Mesh( nodes, triangles, values = 0.1 + 0.9 * h ** 2 )

x = sdot.quantization( target, 6000, seed = 0 )

Reproducing the pages ​

The viewers are written by docs/guide/distributions/codim_mesh_figures.py, which also builds the bottle:

bash
python docs/guide/distributions/codim_mesh_figures.py                  # both
python docs/guide/distributions/codim_mesh_figures.py klein-rho        # or one of them

Limits ​

  • CPU only for now.
  • The smoothed stages are much slower than the measure on M: a first solve from diracs far from M can take minutes on a few thousand triangles. Starting on M avoids them.
  • Points ( a codimension equal to the dimension ) are not meshes: use SumOfGaussians for them.
