Ground Metrics ​

SDOT transports with the cost c(x,y)=|x−y|2. Three independent choices change it, each in its own place:

what changeswhere it is stated
The spaceRd, or its quotient by a group G of isometries: a torus, a Klein bottle, a cone, a crystal... The cost becomes $\min_{g \in G}x - g( y )
The cost of a distance$\varphi(x - y
The mass balanceequal masses, or not: what is not transported goes to a global sink ( partial transport, cells capped by balls of radius wi )target_mass of the distributions

They compose. A periodic space keeps whatever cost and mass balance come with it.

Periodicity ​

periodicity describes a group of isometries G, and the transport takes place on the quotient space Rd/G: two points are the same when an element of G sends one onto the other, and the cost is the squared distance to the nearest image, c(x,y)=ming∈G|x−g(y)|2. It is accepted by plan, w2, ot_solve, OtProblem and PowerDiagram.

The group is given by its generators, each an isometry x↦Mx+V with M orthogonal ( the cost would not be invariant otherwise ). Their inverses and their compositions are added: give the generators, not the group.

a generatorthe map
a vector Vthe translation x↦x+V
a pair ( M, V )x↦Mx+V: a rotation, a symmetry, a glide, a screw...
a (d+1)2 homogeneous matrixthe same, as one matrix
python
import numpy as np
import sdot

flip, quarter = np.diag( [ 1, -1 ] ), np.array( [ [ 0, -1 ], [ 1, 0 ] ] )
f, g = sdot.SumOfDiracs( positions ), sdot.Box()

sdot.plan( f, g, periodicity = [ [ 1, 0 ], [ 0, 1 ] ] )                 # a torus
sdot.plan( f, g, periodicity = [ ( flip, [ 1, 1 ] ), [ 0, 1 ] ] )       # a Klein bottle: across x, the square comes back upside down
sdot.plan( f, g, periodicity = [ ( flip, [ 1, 1 ] ) ] )                 # a Moebius strip ( its other two sides stay walls )
sdot.plan( f, g, periodicity = [ ( quarter, [ 0, 0 ] ) ] )              # a cone: a quarter turn about a corner
sdot.plan( f, g, periodicity = [ [ 1, 1 ] ] )                           # a cylinder along any direction

Two shortcuts read the support of the target:

  • True, or one boolean per axis ( [ True, False ]: a cylinder along x ): one translation per side of the support. This needs an axis-aligned box. For any other domain, or a cylinder along any other direction, give the generators: translations, or ( M, V ) pairs and matrices, glue any domain;
  • target.domain: the half-spaces of the support, whose opposite parallel facets are paired by translations.

The target is repeated by the group: its support must lie in a fundamental domain, so that its images do not overlap ( checked for a lattice of translations ). Its sides that the group does not glue stay walls: those of a cylinder, a Moebius strip, a cone. The diracs can lie anywhere, and each cell is the whole cell of its dirac as given: not cut at the sides of the domain, possibly outside it, away from the others. The barycenters and the moments are those of these cells, so 2 m ( p - b ) ( cost_and_position_grad ) stays continuous when a dirac crosses a side. PowerDiagram.add_to_viz( viz ) draws the cells with their copies by the first ring of the group ( nb_periodicity_rings, periodicity_rings ), and the domain dashed: see the example.

Everything is differentiable as without periodicity: with respect to the positions, the masses of the diracs and the parameters of the target. The example shows the cells on a torus, a Klein bottle, a Moebius strip, a cone, a wallpaper group, a hexagonal lattice and a turned cylinder.

How it works ​

There is one cell per dirac. It is built around its dirac, in the plane, and is not cut at the sides of the support. A cell cannot leave the Voronoi cell of its dirac among its own images, since they all have the same weight.

Internally, the diracs are first folded onto the support by the group ( sdot.wrap does the same thing by hand ). This changes nothing in what is computed, since a dirac and its images are the same point of the quotient, and the cells come back next to the diracs as given ( an isometry ). What it buys is compactness. The differences between the diracs stay within a period, so the images that can cut a cell are a short, fixed list for all the cells: 9 translations on a 2D torus, however far apart the given diracs are. Each cell then starts from one box, the support widened by half a period. Without folding, the list of images would have to cover the spread of the diracs, and so would that box. For the translations of the group, a lattice, this bounds the cells, whatever the weights: the cells start from the support widened by half a period ( exactly half a period for a rectangular lattice ).

The planes come from the diracs and from their images by the elements of the group that come close to the support. The tree of the diracs is traversed once per element, with its boxes moved. The density is integrated as the sum of its images, each cutting the cell into its own pieces. Cost: in 2D, a torus traverses the tree 9 times instead of once, but the pruning drops most of the traversals right away.

On a GPU ​

The diagrams ( PowerDiagram: measures, moments, their derivatives ) work on a CUDA device for any group. The solve on a CUDA device runs on the card's own cells, which walk the images of a group of translations ( a torus, a cylinder, a sheared lattice ) with a constant density ( a Box, a Polytope, an Image of equal values ), in 2D and 3D. A group with rotations or symmetries, or a density that is not a constant, is solved on the CPU ( LOOM_DEVICE=cpu ).

What is not supported yet ​

  • targets other than Image, Box and Polytope ( SumOfGaussians, Mesh );
  • 1D ( the circle is another algorithm ), the "limits" Newton step ( "trials" is used ), the width continuation of an image ( the solve starts from the sharp image ).

Partial transport ​

The masses of the distributions decide whether the transport is partial: each one is normalized to its target_mass ( 1 by default, None for its own mass ). When the source is lighter than the target, the diracs take what is nearest to them, and the rest of the target goes to a sink:

python
sdot.plan( sdot.SumOfDiracs( positions, target_mass = 0.4 ), sdot.Box() )     # 40 % of the box is transported

The sink is a seed of weight 0 that is at the same power distance from every point, so a point x goes to dirac i when |x−pi|2−wi is the smallest power distance and is negative. The cell of i is then its Laguerre cell intersected with the ball Bi of radius wi, and the cost is ∑i∫Li∩Bi|x−pi|2ρ. Two equal target_mass values balance exactly; otherwise the masses are compared up to a relative 10−12.

The barycenters, the cost, the weights and their derivatives ( with respect to the positions, the masses of the diracs and the parameters of the target ) are those of the partial transport. cost_and_position_grad is still 2mi(pi−bi). The example shows the balls as the mass of the source grows, and Lloyd's iterations on two bumps.

How it works ​

The cells are not changed: they stay the Laguerre cells, and it is the integration that is restricted to the balls. A distribution still cuts a cell into its pieces of constant density. Each piece crossing the sphere is then integrated against ρ1Bi in closed form ( a polygon inter a disc: triangles and circular sectors ), with its facets, its moments and their adjoints. The diagram takes it as PowerDiagram( ..., sink_weight = 0 ) ( integration/InBall.h ). Other integrands, such as the radial costs of the roadmap, take the same place.

For Newton, the sink is one more seed, whose target mass is what the diracs leave of the target and whose weight, 0, is the gauge. Its facet with dirac i is the arc of the sphere inside the cell, ∫∂Bi∩Liρ/(2wi). The Hessian stays a graph Laplacian, and the solver and its linear solvers are those of the balanced transport. The start is a ball per dirac, of half its target mass.

What is not supported yet ​

  • a source heavier than the target ( an explicit error ), and the penalized variant ( a sink of fixed weight );
  • 3D, targets that are not constant on pieces ( SumOfGaussians, Mesh ), a periodicity, the CUDA solve, the "limits" step.
