Partial Transport ​

When the source weighs less than the target, only part of the target is transported, and the rest goes to a sink. The masses decide: each distribution is normalized to its target_mass ( 1 by default ).

python
p = sdot.plan( sdot.SumOfDiracs( positions, target_mass = 0.4 ), sdot.Box() )     # 40 % of the box is transported

Two equal target_mass values balance exactly; otherwise the masses are compared up to a relative 10−12. With target_mass = None, a distribution weighs what it holds: the sum of the weights of the diracs, or the integral of the density of an image. For instance, 30 diracs of uneven masses summing to 0.45 against the unit square take 45% of it, a heavy dirac taking a bigger ball than a light one:

python
masses = rng.uniform( 0.2, 1, size = 30 ) ** 2
masses *= 0.45 / masses.sum()
p = sdot.plan( sdot.SumOfDiracs( positions, masses, target_mass = None ), sdot.Box() )

What each dirac takes ​

The sink has weight 0: a point x goes to dirac i when |x−pi|2−wi is the smallest power distance and is negative. In 1D, with three diracs ( weights chosen by hand ): each point goes to the lowest of the parabolas, or to the sink when they are all above 0. The interval of a dirac stops either where another parabola gets lower ( a Laguerre facet ) or where its own parabola crosses 0, at a distance wi from it:

The cell of i is its Laguerre cell intersected with the ball Bi of radius wi, and the cost is ∑i∫Li∩Bi|x−pi|2ρ.

Visualization ​

The Laguerre cells are computed as usual. Only the integration is restricted to the balls, so add_to_viz draws the cells whole but faded ( opacity_beyond_balls ), with what each dirac transports in full color on top:

python
viz = sdot.Visualizer()
p.add_to_viz( viz )
viz.write_html( "partial.html" )

The mass of the source ​

40 diracs on a density in bands, the same positions, a source whose mass goes from 0.15 to 1 ( the slider at the top of the viewer ). At low mass the balls are disjoint, smaller where the density is high ( the same mass on a smaller area ). As the mass grows they meet, and the Laguerre facets split them; at a mass of 1 the transport is balanced, and the cells fill the square.

Under the hood ​

The cells are not changed: they stay the Laguerre cells, and it is the integration that is restricted to the balls, in closed form ( a polygon inter a disc, for a density constant or affine on it: an Image, a Box, a Mesh ), with the derivatives. In 3D, a polyhedron inter a ball, for a density constant on it ( an Image, a Box, a Mesh( degree = 0 ) ): by the divergence theorem, the faces inter their discs ( the 2D closed form, in the plane of each face ) and the solid angles of the sphere inside the cell ( integration/InBall3.h ). For Newton, the sink is one more seed, whose target mass is what the diracs leave of the target and whose weight, 0, is the gauge: the solver and its linear solvers are those of the balanced transport. The diagram takes it as PowerDiagram( ..., sink_weight = 0 ).

What is not supported yet ​

  • a source heavier than the target ( an explicit error ), and the penalized variant ( a sink of fixed weight );
  • targets that are neither constant nor affine on pieces ( SumOfGaussians, a polynomial Image ), and in 3D the affine ones too ( a Mesh of degree 1: Mesh( degree = 0 ) is constant on each tetrahedron ); the CUDA solve of an Image that is not a constant, the "limits" step.

On a GPU ​

The solve runs on a CUDA device in 2D for a constant density ( a Box, a Polytope ) or a Mesh, with or without a group of translations: the cells are integrated in their balls on the card, and the linear systems, bordered by the sink, are solved on the host ( the CPU's solver, on a copy of the laplacian at each iteration ).
